# Navigating the Process After a Loss

When someone passes away, there is often a formal process to settle their affairs and pass on what they owned. While many people use the term "probate," we like to think of it as "The Handover."

### What is "The Handover"?
It is a step-by-step process to make sure:
1.  All the person's final bills are paid.
2.  Any taxes they owed are taken care of.
3.  Their belongings and money go exactly where they wanted them to.

### How Long Does it Take?
Because this process usually involves a court, it can be slow—often taking 6 months to a year. This is why having a "Family Readiness Plan" is so important; it helps your family navigate this time with a clear map.

### Can We Make it Faster?
Yes. Many things can bypass the court process entirely:
- **Joint Accounts:** If a bank account is in two names, it usually stays with the survivor.
- **Named Recipients:** Life insurance and many retirement accounts go directly to the person named on the policy.
- **Small Estates:** Many states have a "fast track" process if the person didn't own a lot of property.

### Your Next Step
If you are currently helping a family through this, your first step is to **find the original Will** (if there was one). This document names the "Executor"—the person who will lead the process.
