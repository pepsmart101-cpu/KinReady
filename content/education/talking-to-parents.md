# How to Talk to Your Parents About Their Finances

One of the hardest conversations you’ll ever have with your parents isn't about their health—it’s about their money. For many, finances are deeply private. But talking about them now is the only way to ensure their wishes are honored later.

Here is how to approach the "money talk" with empathy and clarity.

### 1. Don't Wait for a Crisis
The worst time to talk about bank accounts is in a hospital waiting room. Start the conversation while everyone is healthy and calm. Frame it as part of your own planning: *"I’ve been getting my own 'family readiness' plan in order, and it made me realize I don’t know what your plan looks like."*

### 2. Focus on "What," Not "How Much"
You don't need to know their exact net worth. You need to know the logistics. Focus on:
- **Where:** Where do they keep their important documents?
- **Who:** Who are their trusted advisors (lawyer, accountant, banker)?
- **How:** How do they pay their monthly bills? Is everything on auto-pay?

### 3. Use the "Gift" Approach
Remind your parents that sharing this information is a gift to you. It ensures that if something happens, you won't have to spend weeks playing detective. *"I want to make sure I can support you exactly the way you want if you ever need me to. Knowing where things are would be a huge weight off my shoulders."*

### 4. Start Small
You don't have to cover everything in one sitting. Start with one simple question:
- *"If you were in an accident today, where would I find the info to pay your electric bill so the lights stay on?"*
- *"Do you have a 'Next-of-Kin' contact listed on your main bank account?"*

### 5. Offer to Help Organize
Sometimes parents are overwhelmed by their own paperwork. Offer to help them set up a "Readiness Folder" or a digital vault like KinReady. This turns a scary conversation into a collaborative project.

**Remember:** Your goal isn't to take control; it’s to be ready to support them when the time comes.
